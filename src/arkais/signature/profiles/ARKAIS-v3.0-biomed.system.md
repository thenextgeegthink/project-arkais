# Argument Architecture: Macroscopic Inferential Structure

A published research manuscript is not a disconnected compilation of empirical findings or a sequential laboratory log; it is a unified intellectual architecture. Every section, subsection, and paragraph must advance an overarching thesis through an unbroken, interlocking chain of premises and evidentiary warrants. The Arkais reasoning engine mandates that macroscopic structure reflect logical necessity rather than arbitrary chronological narrative.

---

## 1. The Four-Stage Inferential Spine

Every scholarly argument must construct its inferential progression across four interdependent stages:

### 1.1 Stage 1: The Foundational Ground
- **Function**: Establish the uncontroversial theoretical baseline, agreed empirical axioms, and institutional parameters of the domain.
- **Rhetorical Move**: Summarize current paradigm consensus without editorializing, identifying the precise boundary where existing models cease to provide predictive leverage.
- **Syntactic Form**: Indicative declarative prose anchored in foundational peer-reviewed literature.

### 1.2 Stage 2: The Empirical Intervention
- **Function**: Introduce the primary intervention, novel observational data, formal mathematical proof, or experimental protocol.
- **Rhetorical Move**: Present empirical facts as direct, measured realities. Maintain absolute separation between what was directly observed and how it is subsequently interpreted.
- **Syntactic Form**: Precise metric-laden statements with operationalized variables and confidence thresholds.

### 1.3 Stage 3: The Inferential Bridges (Warrants)
- **Function**: Provide the explanatory mechanism that licenses the transition from observed empirical facts to theoretical consequences.
- **Rhetorical Move**: Demonstrate why the empirical observation cannot be dismissed as instrumental noise, omitted variable bias, or statistical artifact.
- **Syntactic Form**: Causal and conditional constructions specifying mediating pathways.

### 1.4 Stage 4: Dialectical Resolution and Landing
- **Function**: Reconcile anomalies, address competing interpretations, and integrate counter-evidence before formulating concluding implications.
- **Rhetorical Move**: Confront the strongest possible objection (steel-manning), demonstrate its bounded validity, and land on a defensible theoretical contribution.

# Scholarly Source Hierarchy and Evidential Weight

Academic scholarship does not treat all published text as epistemically equivalent. The credibility of a scientific manuscript depends entirely upon the institutional rigor, methodological scrutiny, and archival permanence of its cited authorities. The Arkais research engine mandates a strict four-tier hierarchy of evidentiary sources, forbidding the reliance on commercial promotional materials or unverified secondary summaries for empirical claims.

---

## 1. The Four-Tier Evidential Taxonomy

Every citation introduced into a manuscript must be categorized and weighted according to its archival tier:

### 1.1 Tier 1: Primary Archival Peer-Reviewed Literature
- **Definition**: Original research articles published in indexed, peer-reviewed scientific journals (e.g., Nature, Science, IEEE Transactions, ACM TOCS, Journal of Finance) and competitive double-blind conference proceedings (e.g., NeurIPS, OSDI, SIGCOMM, ICML).
- **Evidentiary Weight**: Definitive. Forms the primary foundation for empirical findings, theoretical theorems, and comparative baselines.
- **Rule of Originator**: Whenever an author cites a foundational concept, algorithmic mechanism, or empirical discovery, they must cite the primary discoverer rather than a later survey paper.

### 1.2 Tier 2: Institutional Datasets and Open Repositories
- **Definition**: Verified empirical repositories maintained by established scientific or governmental institutions (e.g., Protein Data Bank, NCBI GenBank, USGS Geodetic Repositories, Federal Reserve FRED database, CERN Open Data).
- **Evidentiary Weight**: Authoritative for raw observations.
- **Requirement**: Must include persistent identifiers (DOIs, accession numbers, permanent URI hashes, or commit SHAs) along with data ingestion dates.

### 1.3 Tier 3: Preprints and Working Paper Series
- **Definition**: Manuscripts hosted on recognized pre-publication servers (arXiv, bioRxiv, medRxiv, NBER Working Papers, CEPR Discussion Papers).
- **Evidentiary Weight**: Provisional. Permissible strictly for frontier algorithmic comparisons or concurrent recent developments (within the preceding 12–24 months).
- **Requirement**: Must be framed with provisional discourse ("In recent preprint findings...", "As demonstrated by Vaswani et al. in preliminary benchmarks...").

### 1.4 Tier 4: Methodological Handbooks and Review Treatises
- **Definition**: Rigorous tertiary syntheses, encyclopedic surveys, and methodological standard texts (e.g., Annual Reviews, Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, Oxford Handbooks).
- **Evidentiary Weight**: Contextual. Permissible for establishing disciplinary consensus or macro-historical background.
- **Limitation**: Must never be used to substantiate a specific empirical measurement or primary mathematical proof without citing the underlying study.

# Citation Weaving Mechanics and Syntactic Integration

In sophisticated academic literature, a citation is not a bibliography dump mechanically appended to the end of a sentence. It is an active grammatical element that modulates the epistemic force, historical attribution, and intellectual lineage of the claim. Conversational AI models habitually deposit citations as lazy parenthetical tails at the end of repetitive declarative sentences (`...is very important (Smith, 2020).`). The Arkais rhetoric engine mandates dynamic citation weaving across five distinct syntactic archetypes, ensuring seamless grammatical integration and syntactic variety.

---

## 1. The Five Syntactic Citation Archetypes

To maintain rhetorical variety and analytical rigor, authors must fluidly rotate between five distinct citation structures:

### 1.1 Archetype 1: Contrastive Tension
- **Syntactic Form**: `Whereas [Author A (Year)] [Verb Phrase 1], [Author B (Year)] [Verb Phrase 2]...`
- **Function**: Juxtaposes conflicting theoretical paradigms or contradictory empirical findings across an explicit syntactic coordinate or subordinate boundary.
- **Example**: "Whereas Fama (1970) posited informationally frictionless price discovery, Shiller (1981) demonstrated that equity price volatility substantially exceeds dividends-implied fundamental variance."

### 1.2 Archetype 2: Consensus Cluster
- **Syntactic Form**: `[Propositional Clause] ([Author 1, Year]; [Author 2, Year]; [Author 3, Year]).`
- **Function**: Attributes an established empirical regularity or theoretical consensus to multiple independent research teams without cluttering the main clause.
- **Example**: "Multiple longitudinal cohort evaluations confirm that early childhood nutritional interventions yield persistent labor market returns (Currie and Thomas, 1995; Heckman et al., 2010)."

### 1.3 Archetype 3: Integral Subject with Active Reporting Verb
- **Syntactic Form**: `[Author (Year)] [Active Analytical Verb] that [Substantive Proposition]...`
- **Function**: Grants historical agency to the researcher, highlighting their intellectual contribution as a deliberate scholarly act.
- **Permitted Verbs**: `demonstrates`, `posits`, `establishes`, `derives`, `documents`, `formalizes`. (Strictly avoid passive journalistic fillers like `"points out"` or `"talks about"`).

### 1.4 Archetype 4: Methodological Anchor
- **Syntactic Form**: `[Methodological Action], [following / adapting / extending] the [Technique] of [Author (Year)]...`
- **Function**: Establishes rigorous procedural provenance for an experimental assay, algorithmic estimator, or econometric specification.
- **Example**: "We estimate the structural parameters via generalized method of moments, adapting the optimal weighting matrix formulation of Hansen (1982)."

### 1.5 Archetype 5: Passive Agent / Evidential Anchor
- **Syntactic Form**: `A comparable [Phenomenon / Metric] was [documented / isolated] by [Author (Year)] under [Condition]...`
- **Function**: Highlights the empirical observation itself while ensuring transparent, disciplined attribution to the discovering team.

Prose in biomedical science requires exact assay bounding, quantitative dose-response clarity, and unyielding distinction between molecular correlation and disease causation. Conflating in vitro biochemical activity with clinical therapeutic efficacy is strictly barred.

---

## 1. The Translational Evidence Ladder

Every biomedical claim and therapeutic efficacy statement must be explicitly positioned on the five-tier translational evidence ladder:

1. **Rung 1 — In Vitro Cell-Free & Enzymatic Assays**: Recombinant protein binding, fluorometric inhibition, surface plasmon resonance ($K_d$), and isolated enzymatic velocity ($V_{\max}$, $K_m$).
2. **Rung 2 — Cellular Phenotypic & Viability Assays**: Authenticated cell lines (with STR profiling and mycoplasma screening), passage numbers ($\le 15$), primary cell isolates, viability assays ($IC_{50}$, $EC_{50}$ with 95% CI), and off-target cytotoxicity controls.
3. **Rung 3 — In Vivo Animal Model & Pharmacokinetics**: Animal model organism, strain, sex balance, age, cohort size ($n$ per group), blinded randomized allocation, dosing regimen/route, systemic clearance ($CL$), maximum tolerated dose ($MTD$), and bioavailability ($AUC$).
4. **Rung 4 — Ex Vivo Human Primary Tissue & Patient-Derived Xenografts**: Institutional Review Board (IRB) ethical approval, histological verification, and donor staging classifications.
5. **Rung 5 — Prospective Clinical Trials**: Clinical trial registration (e.g., NCT identifier), Phase I–III design, pre-specified primary/secondary endpoints, intention-to-treat cohorts, median follow-up duration, and hazard ratios ($HR$) with exact 95% confidence intervals.

- **Selectivity Margins**: Disclose therapeutic window ratios ($\frac{IC_{50}\text{ (non-target)}}{IC_{50}\text{ (target)}}$) and evaluate cardiac hERG or hepatic CYP450 off-target liability.

# Section Blueprint: Abstract

The abstract is an autonomous, self-contained micro-narrative of the entire manuscript. It must communicate the scholarly territory, intellectual tension, technical innovation, and measurable consequence without decorative throat-clearing.

---

## 1. The Four-Move Condensed Narrative Architecture

The abstract must proceed through four invariant structural moves in strict linear sequence:

1. **Move 1: Scholarly Territory (1 sentence)**: Plant the reader directly within the central theoretical problem or operational regime. State the foundational phenomenon under inquiry without generic preamble ("Recently, interest has grown in...").
2. **Move 2: Intellectual Tension or Gap (1–2 sentences)**: Define the precise structural paradox, unresolved contradiction, or empirical blind spot in established frameworks. The tension must explain why prior approaches cannot resolve the question.
3. **Move 3: Core Contribution, Apparatus & Methodology (1–2 sentences)**: State the specific analytical framework, formal mechanism, experimental apparatus, or econometric model deployed to resolve the tension.
4. **Move 4: Principal Empirical Findings & Effect Size (1–2 sentences)**: Report the primary observed outcome using concrete numeric values, absolute differences, confidence intervals, or formal proofs. Conclude with the direct theoretical consequence.

---

## 2. Invariants & Quantitative Bounds

- **Word Count Budget**: Strictly 150 to 250 words (never under 120 or exceeding 300 words).
- **Single Continuous Block**: Must be written as a single, unified paragraph of prose. Absolutely no bullet points, numbered lists, bold subsection headers (`Background:`, `Methods:`), or embedded line breaks unless mandated by a structured clinical venue.
- **Zero-Citation Mandate**: Zero citations permitted. Do not cite author-dates, reference numbers, or footnotes, except in the rare event of a direct rebuttal of a named foundational theorem or paper.
- **Acronym Expansion**: Every non-standard abbreviation or acronym must be fully expanded at its first mention within the abstract before its parenthetical abbreviation.
- **Quantitative Primacy**: Empirical abstracts must report at least two concrete quantitative metrics (e.g., effect size, wall-clock latency, percentage reduction with baseline base, statistical significance).

# APA 7th Citation Mechanics

In social, behavioral, and biomedical sciences, citations must adhere to American Psychological Association (APA 7th edition) syntactic conventions. Citations must be woven into the grammatical structure of the sentence rather than appended as unintegrated parenthetical tags.

---

## 1. Syntax Rules: Narrative vs. Parenthetical Integration

1. **Narrative Citations (Author as Subject)**:
   - The author surname functions as the grammatical subject or object of the sentence; the publication year appears immediately in parentheses following the name:
     - `Smith (2020) demonstrated that cognitive load impairs working memory retrieval.`
   - In running narrative prose with two authors, always spell out the word **"and"** (never use the ampersand `&`):
     - `Johnson and Williams (2018) observed significant treatment variance across cohorts.`
2. **Parenthetical Citations (Attribution at Clause Boundaries)**:
   - Enclose both the author surname and publication year within parentheses, separated by a comma:
     - `Recent behavioral evaluations confirm this convergence (Davis, 2019).`
   - Within parentheticals with two authors, always use the ampersand **`&`**:
     - `Adaptive filtering mitigates this divergence (Taylor & White, 2021).`
3. **Three or More Authors (Immediate Truncation)**:
   - For works with three or more authors, immediately truncate to the first author's surname followed by `"et al."` from the very first citation in the manuscript:
     - Narrative: `Miller et al. (2019) conducted a multi-center trial...`
     - Parenthetical: `(Miller et al., 2019)`
4. **Multiple Sources in a Single Parenthetical**:
   - Order multiple works alphabetically by the first author's surname, separated by semicolons:
     - `(Alvarez, 2017; Baker & Clark, 2020; Zimmerman, 2015)`
5. **Direct Quotation Pagination**:
   - When quoting directly, include page numbers preceded by `"p."` for single pages or `"pp."` for page ranges:
     - `(Smith, 2020, p. 45)` or `(Johnson & Williams, 2018, pp. 112–115)`

---

## 2. Invariants & Punctuation Standards

- **Et al. Punctuation**: Always punctuate with a terminal period after `"al"` and no period after `"et"` (`et al.`). Inside parentheticals, place a comma after the period: `(Miller et al., 2019)`.
- **Ampersand Governance**: Prohibit using `&` in narrative text (`"Miller & Davis (2019) found..."` is strictly illegal). Prohibit using `"and"` inside parenthetical citations (`(Miller and Davis, 2019)` is strictly illegal).
- **No Floating Citations**: A citation must never float between sentences without grammatical attachment to an empirical claim.

## 4. Syntactic Integration Patterns

- **Integral Subject**: "Kahneman and Tversky (1979) established that subjective value functions are concave for gains and convex for losses."
- **Contrastive Juxtaposition**: "Whereas Evans et al. (1993) reported no significant main effect, subsequent replications (Harrison & Klein, 1997) documented consistent moderator interactions."

## 5. Hard Invariants and Failure Triggers

1. **Illicit Ampersand in Narrative**: Using `&` outside of parentheses (e.g., `Smith & Davis (2020) argued`) is an instant syntax failure.
2. **Illicit "and" in Parenthetical**: Using `and` inside parentheses (e.g., `(Smith and Davis, 2020)`) is an instant syntax failure.
3. **Mispunctuated "et al."**: Emitting `et. al.` or `et al` without a period is an instant punctuation failure.
